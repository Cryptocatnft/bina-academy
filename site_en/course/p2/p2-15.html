Portfolio Project 3: Content Pipeline

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Build an end-to-end automated content pipeline — from idea to researched draft to published post — the project that markets you while you sleep.
Alex · Play the lecturer

Step 1: The content engine (45 min)

Define the pipeline's contract: input — a topic row in a spreadsheet (topic, audience, angle, keywords); output — a publish-ready draft. Build the stages:

1. Research: agent searches the web for 3-5 recent, real sources on the topic (hallucination rules apply — sources must be verifiable).

2. Draft: system message with a voice file — 2-3 of your best writing samples as few-shot examples, so every piece sounds like you, not like AI.

3. Structure: a fixed output format per channel — a LinkedIn post under 200 words with a hook line, or a blog outline with H2s.

4. Review: a separate AI pass that checks voice match, factual claims, and banned-words list ("delve," "tapestry," and friends).

Each stage = one node in n8n/Make, or one section in a Custom GPT's instructions.

Step 2: The human approval gate (30 min)

Rule of professional content pipelines: AI drafts, human approves, automation publishes. The middle step exists because publishing is a reputational, irreversible action — the exact category Lesson 12 fenced off. Implement it simply: drafts land in a spreadsheet, Notion board, or email with an approve/reject column or buttons. Approved items flow to scheduling (Buffer, native LinkedIn scheduling, or your platform's scheduler); rejected ones return with a one-line reason that feeds back into the voice file. Track the loop's stats — drafts per week, approval rate, edits needed per draft. An improving approval rate is a number that impresses employers and clients more than any adjective.

Step 3: Run it for a week (30 min + waiting)

Load 10 topic rows and let the machine run end-to-end. Your job now is editor, not writer: review each draft, approve or reject with reasons, and watch the quality of the batch. Expect to reject your first two or three — that's data, not failure. Patch the voice file with one contrasting example (bad vs. good), tighten the banned-words list, re-run. By batch two, approval rates typically climb sharply. Then publish 2-3 of the best pieces on your own LinkedIn or blog — you now have a working pipeline *and* its output as evidence. This is the most self-marketing artifact a job seeker can own.

Step 4: Document and demo (30 min)

The README writes the story employers want to hear: problem ('consistent content takes hours'), solution (an automated pipeline with a human approval gate), architecture (a simple diagram: sheet → research agent → drafter → reviewer → approval → scheduler), results (drafts per week, your approval rate, hours saved — even from one week of data), decisions (where the human gate is and why), limits and next steps. Record a 60-second demo video of one topic flowing through. That's portfolio artifact #3 — and unlike most portfolio pieces, it produces *ongoing* evidence: your feed fills up while you interview. Machine marketing.

Copy-ready prompt

Draft a [CHANNEL: LinkedIn post / blog section] about [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE]. Voice rules: match the tone of these two samples: [PASTE 2 SAMPLES]. Include one concrete example and one number from research. Banned words: delve, tapestry, unlock, game-changer. End with a question. Under 200 words.

Act as a strict content reviewer. Check this draft against my voice samples: [PASTE SAMPLES + DRAFT]. Score voice match, originality, and factual risk 1-10 each, list the 3 weakest lines, and mark any claim needing a source. Do not rewrite — I'll do that.

💪 Exercise

Ship the full pipeline: spreadsheet of 10 topics, all four stages wired, approval gate enforced, one week of runs logged with stats. Publish at least 2 approved pieces under your own name. Add README + 60-second demo video to your portfolio folder.
