Project 2: Document Analyzer with RAG

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Build a document-intelligence app — upload documents, extract structured fields, ask grounded questions with page-level citations — and deploy it publicly.
Alex · Play the lecturer

What makes it valuable

Support agents deflect tickets; document analyzers save professional hours — reviewing a contract, auditing invoices, summarizing reports. That's budget-line value, which is why 'document AI' and 'intelligent document processing' appear in a huge slice of real postings. Your version: a user uploads PDFs or text files; the app parses them, chunks on structure (pages, sections, tables — your Lesson 9 chunking opinions finally matter), embeds into a per-user collection, and offers two modes: Q&A with [page N] citations, and extraction into Pydantic schemas (e.g. from an invoice: vendor, amounts, dates, line items). Same primitives, productized.

The pipeline

Architecture in one flow:

`

upload → parse (pypdf) → chunk + metadata(page, section)

→ embed → Chroma collection per document

Q&A: question → retrieve top-5 → grounded answer + citations

Extract: schema + retrieve relevant chunks → Pydantic-validated fields

`

Two details that separate you from every 'chat with PDF' demo:

1. Citations are clickable: the answer links to the retrieved chunk and its page number, displayed in the UI. Verifiability is the product.

2. Extraction is validated: extraction is a per-field confidence score — and low-confidence fields are flagged, not silently guessed.

That's grounded, verifiable, production-shaped thinking — exactly what an AI-application interview probes for.

Make it demo-able

A document tool with no documents is a car with no gas. Ship it with: two pre-loaded public sample documents (a real open-source license agreement, a sample invoice), a drag-and-drop upload box, and a side panel showing retrieved chunks for every answer. Keep the stack honest and clean: FastAPI + your RAG engine + a single-page frontend (plain HTML/JS or Streamlit — the point is the pipeline, not the pixels). Reuse Lesson 10's Docker and deploy loop. Evals: a 20-question golden set on the sample docs with expected citations — faithfulness scored by your Lesson 12 judge. README shows the eval table next to the demo link.

Enterprise signals to bake in

Small touches that make engineers and hiring managers nod: per-document collections deleted when a user removes the doc (data hygiene); a file-type and size guard with clear errors; scan-friendly text extraction with a fallback note when a PDF is image-only; and a cost readout (tokens per question, roughly in dollars). None of these are hard; all of them are what 'production mindset' means concretely. In your README, write one paragraph titled 'what I'd do at 10x scale': batch embedding, a job queue, pgvector, per-tenant isolation. That paragraph is a conversation starter in every interview — it shows you see past the demo.

Copy-ready prompt

Design a Pydantic extraction schema for [DOCUMENT TYPE: invoice / contract / lab report] with per-field descriptions that double as model instructions. Include confidence and a required source-page reference per field.

My document analyzer retrieves the wrong chunks for table-heavy PDFs. Give me a prioritized improvement plan: parsing, chunking strategy, table handling, query rewriting, hybrid search. One experiment per item.

Write the 'what I'd do at 10x scale' section of my README: assume 10k documents and 100 concurrent users. Cover storage, embedding jobs, queues, cost, and per-tenant isolation — in confident, concrete engineer voice.
