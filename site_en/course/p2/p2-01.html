How LLMs Actually Work (in Plain Words)

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Understand what really happens inside a large language model so you can predict when AI will shine and when it will fail.
Alex · Play the lecturer

The world's best autocomplete

An LLM generates text one token (a word chunk) at a time. Given everything so far, it computes a probability distribution over possible next tokens and picks one — usually a good one, sometimes a lucky one. It repeats this until it decides to stop. There is no lookup table of facts and no internal fact-checker. When a model answers your question, it is generating text that *looks like* a good answer, based on patterns absorbed during training. That's why it can write brilliant poetry and confidently invent a fake citation in the same breath.

Training: reading (most of) the internet

Models are trained in stages. Pretraining: the model reads enormous amounts of text and learns to predict the next token — this is where its knowledge of language, code, and the world comes from. Fine-tuning: humans rate model outputs, teaching it to be helpful and follow instructions. Alignment: safety and behavior tuning. Practical takeaway: the model's knowledge is frozen at training time, which is why it may not know about last month's news unless it has web search or you paste the information in.

Why this explains everything

Once you see the model as a pattern-completion engine, its quirks become predictable:

  • It hallucinates because plausible text, not truth, is its target.
  • It forgets earlier parts of a long chat because attention fades over distance.
  • It follows your format because it imitates patterns — including your typos and your clarity.
  • It does better with context — a vague prompt gives the pattern engine too many directions to go.

Great prompt engineering is really just giving the autocomplete machine an easy pattern to complete.

What it is NOT

It is not a search engine, a calculator, or a database — though it can *use* those things when connected to tools, which we'll cover in Module 3. It doesn't "know" it's right, doesn't reason about truth, and doesn't have goals. Treating it like an eager, extremely well-read intern who sometimes bluffs is the right mental model. Your job in this career track: become the person who deploys that intern reliably — with verification, structure, and automation.
