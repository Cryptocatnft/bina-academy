The AI Tool Stack of 2026

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Map the current AI ecosystem — the layers, the names, and what each layer is actually for — so you never feel lost in tool talk again.
Alex · Play the lecturer

Layer 1: The models

At the bottom sit the frontier models: OpenAI's GPT family, Anthropic's Claude, Google's Gemini, plus open-weight models like Llama and Mistral that you can run yourself. Key 2026 distinctions: reasoning models (slower, think step-by-step, great for hard analysis) versus fast models (cheap, instant, great for bulk tasks), and multimodal — text, image, audio, video in and out. You don't buy a model directly; you access it through a chat app or an API. Model names change monthly; the *categories* barely change at all.

Layer 2: Chat assistants

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot — the consumer face of AI. This is where most people live, and where most people stop. For our purposes, learn the power features: file uploads, image generation, web search, Projects/Custom GPTs (saved instructions plus documents = a mini assistant), and memory. A professional uses a chat assistant the way a carpenter uses a workbench — one surface among many, not the whole workshop. The different assistants have different strengths; professionals keep two or three and match the task to the tool.

Layer 3: APIs and automation platforms

APIs let software call a model directly — this is what powers every AI product you'll ever build, and Lesson 11 covers it hands-on. Automation platforms — Zapier, Make, n8n — connect APIs to everything else: email, spreadsheets, Slack, databases. n8n is the 2026 favorite for AI work because it's powerful, cheap, and self-hostable, and it has a proper AI-agent node. The pattern to remember: *automation platform = the hands, model = the brain*. Almost every AI Ops and automation job posting lives at this layer.

Layer 4: Vertical and coding tools

On top sit specialized tools: coding assistants (GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code), AI features baked into Notion, Office, and Salesforce, support bots, AI sales tools, and so on. You don't need to master all of them. You need to (1) recognize which layer a tool belongs to, (2) pick one automation platform and get genuinely good at it, and (3) stay roughly aware of what's new — 15 minutes a week of AI news is enough. Depth in one stack beats shallow knowledge of twenty.

Copy-ready prompt

Here are 6 AI tools I've heard of: [LIST THEM]. For each one, tell me which layer of the AI stack it belongs to (model / chat assistant / API+automation / vertical tool), and one realistic use case. Present as a markdown table.

I want to use AI to automate [YOUR REPETITIVE TASK AT WORK OR HOME]. Recommend a minimal tool stack for a beginner, with a 3-step starting plan. Keep it under 200 words.
