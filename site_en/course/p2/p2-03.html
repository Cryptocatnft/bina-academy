Context Windows and Why AI 'Forgets'

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Understand tokens and context windows, and learn the practical techniques that keep long AI conversations and documents from falling apart.
Alex · Play the lecturer

Tokens: the unit of memory

Models don't read words — they read tokens, chunks of text roughly three-quarters of a word in English (Hebrew usually costs more tokens per word). The context window is everything the model can "see" at once: your instructions, the conversation history, pasted documents, and its own previous answers. In 2026, windows range from about 128K tokens (roughly a 300-page book) to over a million on some models. Sounds huge — but everything you *add* eats space, and every reply accumulates. Long chats die by a thousand crumbs.

Lost in the middle

Here's the catch nobody puts on the marketing page: models are strongest with information at the beginning and end of their context, and noticeably weaker with the middle. Stuff 200 pages into a model and ask about page 97, and quality drops — sometimes badly. Bigger windows made "just paste it all in" possible; it did not make it *good*. Professionals treat the context window like a whiteboard: put the important stuff at the top, keep it tidy, and erase what you no longer need.

Three techniques that fix forgetting

1. Summarize and restart. When a long chat starts drifting, ask: *"Summarize our decisions, constraints, and open questions in a compact block."* Start a fresh chat and paste that block in. You keep the knowledge, drop the noise.

2. Compress with standing instructions. Keep a short, fixed block — your goals, format rules, key facts — at the top of every chat, and reference it instead of re-explaining.

3. Retrieve instead of remembering. For large document collections, don't paste everything — fetch only the relevant chunks. That's RAG (retrieval-augmented generation), and you'll build a mini version in Lesson 14.

Reading the signs of context failure

Context problems announce themselves: the model contradicts instructions you gave earlier, re-asks questions it already answered, loses your output format, or drifts back to generic answers. When you spot two of these, don't fight it — restart with a summary. Also watch your costs: API pricing is per token, so sloppy context isn't just a quality problem, it's a money problem. The habit of *deliberate context management* is one of the clearest marks that separates trained AI users from casual ones — and interviewers notice it.

Copy-ready prompt

Summarize this conversation so far into a handoff block for a fresh chat. Include: my goal, decisions made, constraints, current status, and the exact next step. Keep it under 150 words. [PASTE CONVERSATION OR WRITE 'our conversation so far']

Here is a long document: [PASTE DOCUMENT]. Give me: (1) a 5-bullet executive summary, (2) the 3 most important exact quotes with their section numbers, (3) one important detail a reader might miss in the middle of the text.

💪 Exercise

Take a real long document (a report, contract, or article over 10 pages). Paste it into an AI chat and ask a specific question about a detail in the middle. Then apply the summarize-and-restart technique and ask the same question again. Write 3 sentences comparing the two answers — did context management help?
