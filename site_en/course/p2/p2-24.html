Project 5: the personal assistant agent

Your capstone combines everything: build an agent that runs a real part of your week. Minimum scope: (1) inbox triage (Lesson 12's read-only digest), (2) calendar proposals (reads availability, suggests slots, human confirms), (3) document work (summarize attachments, extract key fields to a sheet — reuse your Lesson 16 cleaning skills), (4) a daily briefing — one message each morning: today's meetings, priority emails, open tasks. Architecture: your platform's agent node, a system message with all six blocks, the smallest safe toolset, guardrails from Lesson 12 (read-only where possible, human fence on anything irreversible), and a change/action log. Keep it running for at least five days — real usage data is your results section, and 'I use it daily' is the strongest sentence in any interview.

The exam: what it covers

The completion exam checks working knowledge across all six modules:

  • M1: how LLMs work, context windows, hallucination categories and verification workflow.
  • M2: structured-output schemas, few-shot design, when reasoning pays, system-message blocks.
  • M3: the agent loop, tools and function calling concepts, permission and safety design.
  • M4: the anatomy of your five projects — what you built and the decisions you made.
  • M5: CV keywords and tailoring, LinkedIn zones, portfolio evidence.
  • M6: the job map, interview families, take-home lifecycle.

Format: multiple choice plus short practicals — write a schema, diagnose a broken prompt, spot the hallucination risk. It's open-notes and open-internet, like real work. Study method: reread your own project READMEs and exercise notes; you wrote the exam's answers already, in your own words.

Submission checklist

Submit the following, complete — partial submissions bounce:

1. Final project link or repo — live demo (or screen recording), source, README with architecture, guardrails, five-day usage log, and your design decisions.

2. Test log — minimum 10-message test set with results, including escalation and failure cases.

3. Exam completion — passed within the allowed attempts (you get retakes; treat them as feedback, not failure).

4. Portfolio readiness check — your Lesson 20 site updated with all five projects, links tested.

5. Reflection — 150 words: what you built, what you'd improve, what you'll automate next.

Timeline: most finish project five and the exam within one to two weeks of reaching this lesson. The certificate with its verification number is issued after all five items pass review — the number is what turns the certificate from a PDF into a checkable claim.

After the certificate

Certified, five tested projects, a public portfolio — now run the machine you built in Module 5 and 6: tailored applications to your Lesson 21 target list, weekly mock interviews, weekly posts from your content pipeline. Keep the assistant agent running; keep a build log; interviewers ask 'what have you automated lately?' and you'll have a same-week answer. The field will keep changing — the course's own method (AI builds, AI teaches, you verify) is your method now too: when a new model or tool lands, build a small thing with it, test it like Lesson 4 taught you, write the README. That loop — build, test, document, show — is the career. Verification number in hand, go claim the jobs. — Alex, signing off.
