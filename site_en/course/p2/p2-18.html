LinkedIn Optimization for AI Careers

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Turn LinkedIn into an inbound channel — headline, About section, and activity that surface you to recruiters searching for the exact AI skills you now have.
Alex · Play the lecturer

The headline: your search listing

The headline follows you everywhere and is heavily weighted in recruiter search. Bad: 'Aspiring AI enthusiast.' Good: 'AI Engineer | Python, LangGraph, RAG, MCP | Built & deployed 4 agent systems — demos in Featured'. Formula: target role + core keywords + proof hook. Fill the rest of the profile skeleton the same way: your headline, About, experience titles, and skills field are all searchable — the skills section especially, since recruiters filter by exact skills. Load every true one: Python, LangChain, LangGraph, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), MCP, vector databases, FastAPI, Docker, prompt engineering, LLM evaluation. Each is a door recruiters can find you through.

About: the first two lines are everything

Nobody clicks 'see more' without a reason, so the first two lines must carry the story: 'I build and deploy AI agents — support agents, document analyzers, multi-agent pipelines. Four shipped systems, each with public demos and eval suites.' Then the About earns trust with specifics: the four projects in one line each, with links; the tools; a sentence on how you work (shipped > perfect, measured > guessed); and a clear call to action ('Open to junior AI/software engineering roles — reach me here'). Write it in first person, plain, confident. Then pin your best repo and demo video in the Featured section — profile visitors are one click from proof of your work.

Activity: the compounding signal

A profile gets you found; activity gets you remembered. Sustainable rhythm, 30 minutes a week:

  • One post a week documenting what you build: a screenshot, what it does, one thing you learned. 'Built an MCP server for my assistant — one tool, 20 lines, now any agent client can use it.' Build-in-public posts from your Module 4 work are endless material.
  • Comment daily on posts by AI engineers and companies you'd work for — substantive comments, not 'Great post!'
  • Follow the employers from Lesson 2 (Salesforce, Databricks, OpenAI, Palantir, Citi, Cisco, Anthropic) and their engineering leads.

Recruiters check activity before reaching out. A profile with three months of build-in-public posts reads as 'actively becoming an engineer' — which is precisely what you are.

Settings and network that matter

Practical switches: set 'Open to Work' for the titles you want (recruiter-only visibility if you're employed); turn on 'Signal your interest' to recruiters at target companies; list the BINA certificate in Licenses & Certifications; and use a photo + banner — a simple code-editor screenshot banner beats a default gradient. Networking, uncreepily: when you connect, one personalized line referencing their work, not a job ask. Engage with your cohort's posts — reciprocity is real. And keep your CV's claims and your LinkedIn claims identical: recruiters cross-check, and consistency reads as honesty. Lesson 19 arms the messages you'll send.

Copy-ready prompt

Write 3 LinkedIn headline options for me: target role 'AI / junior software engineer (AI applications)', keywords Python, LangGraph, RAG, MCP, agents. Under 220 characters each, no buzzword soup. My background: [SUMMARIZE].

Draft my LinkedIn About section, first person: four deployed AI projects (support agent, document analyzer, multi-agent pipeline, assistant with MCP), build-in-public style, clear 'open to work' CTA. 150-200 words, plain and confident.

Turn this week's project work into a build-in-public LinkedIn post: [PASTE NOTES]. Hook in the first line, one visual suggestion, one lesson learned, under 150 words, no hashtags beyond 3.

Post your first build-in-public item (screenshot + 4 sentences about a Module 4 project). Then comment substantively on two posts from people at your target companies. Check your profile in an incognito window — would a recruiter searching 'LangGraph' find you?
