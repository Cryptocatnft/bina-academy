Where the Jobs Actually Are: Titles, Employers, Salary, Negotiation

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Run the job hunt like a sales pipeline — search the right titles and employers, know realistic salary bands, and negotiate the first offer without losing it.
Alex · Play the lecturer

Search like a recruiter

Job boards rank and filter by keyword, so search keyword families, not titles: 'Python RAG', 'LangGraph', 'LangChain', 'MCP', 'AI agent developer', 'LLM application', plus the adjacent-entry titles from Lesson 2: junior software engineer (AI), automation engineer, data analyst/engineer, solutions engineer, forward-deployed engineer (junior variants exist — search it directly). Set alerts for five searches on LinkedIn, and add the aggregators for AI-specific boards. Then go around the boards: companies adopting AI list 'AI' in their engineering blogs — a 'careers' page check on 20 such companies beats 200 cold applications. Track everything in a spreadsheet: company, role, date, contact, status, next step.

Who's hiring

Four employer buckets, in order of realistic entry:

1. AI-native companies — Salesforce, Databricks, OpenAI, Palantir, Citi, Cisco, Anthropic and their peers are competing hardest for agentic talent (they anchor the forward-deployed engineer wave). Junior roles are scarce but solutions/engineering-support paths exist.

2. Enterprises adopting AI — banks, insurers, retail, healthcare: thousands of internal AI-application and automation roles. The realistic entry door, and RAG/agents experience maps perfectly.

3. Startups and agencies — fast, title-flexible, proof-over-credentials; your deployed portfolio is exactly their hiring test.

4. In-house translation — your current employer: proposing an AI feature where you already work is often the single fastest path into AI work.

In Israel, add the local angle: a dense startup ecosystem plus multinationals, many hiring for exactly this stack. Local meetups and LinkedIn groups are disproportionately effective there.

Salary: know the band before they ask

Research before numbers: check levels-style sites, LinkedIn salary insights, and — most reliably — ask three people in your market what junior AI-application roles pay; local Facebook/WhatsApp groups and peer networks beat any aggregator. For orientation only: US junior-to-mid AI/software engineering roles commonly span roughly the low-to-mid $100Ks, with total comp higher at AI-native companies; Israeli junior AI/software roles commonly span roughly ₪20K–₪35K monthly depending on seniority-adjacent skills. Treat these as starting points to verify locally — bands move fast, and your deployed portfolio pushes you toward the top of any junior band. When asked for expectations, answer with a researched range and anchor to evidence: 'Based on my research, X to Y — I'm flexible on structure.'

Negotiation without losing the offer

Rules that keep offers alive: never negotiate before intent — first get the offer in writing. Negotiate the band, not a demand: 'Based on my research for this scope, I was expecting closer to X — is there flexibility?' Negotiate one thing primarily (base), but know the other levers: sign-on, start date, education budget, hybrid days. Silence is a tool — after their counter, 'let me think about it overnight' is professional, not rude. And the fallback: if the number won't move, ask for a 6-month review with agreed criteria — 'If I ship X by then, we revisit.' That converts a no into a milestone, and you will ship X. You have receipts for your worth now; use them calmly.

Copy-ready prompt

I'm targeting junior AI-application roles in [YOUR MARKET: e.g., Tel Aviv / US remote]. Build me a 10-search alert strategy: exact search strings, which boards, and how to find the 30% of postings never posted to big boards.

A recruiter asked my salary expectations for a junior AI/software role. Script me: (1) a deflecting first answer, (2) a researched-range answer with my evidence, (3) a polite response if they push for one number. My situation: [SUMMARIZE].

I have an offer: [PASTE DETAILS]. Walk me through deciding: how to compare total comp, what to negotiate first, exact phrasing for the ask, and what not to do.
