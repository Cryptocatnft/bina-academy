Where AI Jobs Actually Are

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Know the real 2026 AI job map — titles, salary ranges, and where beginners actually get hired — and target the right roles for you.
Alex · Play the lecturer

The title map of 2026

The roles that hire people with *your* skillset:

  • AI Automation Specialist / AI Solutions Engineer — build agents and pipelines inside companies (n8n, Zapier, APIs). The most open to non-programmers.
  • AI Ops — keep AI systems running: monitoring, cost control, model routing. Ops people who learn AI are rare and valued.
  • AI Product Manager / AI Product Owner — decide *what* to automate; needs process fluency more than code.
  • Prompt Engineer / AI Content Specialist — narrower now, mostly inside marketing, localization, and support-ops teams.
  • AI Consultant / Fractional Automator — freelance versions of all the above; fastest first dollar.

'AI Engineer' (heavy code) exists too — it's the Python track, not this one, but you'll collaborate with those people constantly.

Realistic salary ranges (with honesty)

Directional 2026 figures — markets vary widely, so verify on local listings:

  • United States: junior AI automation/solutions roles roughly $70-110k; mid-level $110-160k; AI Ops similar, often higher with on-call pay. Prompt-engineer-flavored roles: $60-120k depending on how technical.
  • Israel: junior automation/AI-ops roles roughly 12-18k ILS/month; experienced 20-35k ILS/month; the market is hot and Hebrew/English bilinguals get remote premiums.
  • Remote/global and freelance: project-based automation from a few hundred dollars per build to $50-150/hour for consultants with a visible portfolio.

The consistent pattern across all three: portfolio + numbers beats credentials. Employers test what you built — that's what Module 4 was for.

Where beginners actually get hired

The entry points that work:

1. Your current employer — the #1 path. Volunteer to automate one painful process, ship it, get the title changed or the promotion. Internal converts beat external applicants almost every time.

2. Small and mid-size companies — 20-200 people. No AI team, real automation needs, no patience for credentials theater. They hire people who demo working systems.

3. Agencies and consultancies — automation boutiques and AI consultancies hire juniors who can build and *communicate*.

3. Adjacent moves — support leads becoming AI support specialists; ops people becoming AI Ops; marketers becoming content-automation people. Prior domain + AI skills is the strongest combo in the market.

What rarely works for beginners: applying cold to big-tech 'AI Engineer' roles against CS graduates. Aim where your advantage is real.

Reading job descriptions like a pro

Decode postings before applying:

  • Reality check: 'AI Automation Specialist, must know Python' = hybrid code role. 'n8n/Zapier, workflows, LLM APIs' = your track.
  • Wish-list vs. requirements: job ads are wish-lists; meet ~60% and apply anyway.
  • Translation table: 'prompt engineering' → what you did in Module 2. 'RAG experience' → Lesson 14. 'Agent frameworks' → Modules 3-4. 'Stakeholder communication' → your READMEs and test logs.
  • Filter for testability: postings asking for 'a portfolio or examples of automations built' are *your* postings — they evaluate evidence, not pedigree. Apply to those first.

Build a tracking spreadsheet this week — title, company, source, status, contact. Job search is a pipeline; treat it like one of your projects, with metrics and weekly reviews.

Copy-ready prompt

Here is a job description: [PASTE IT]. Tell me: (1) is this really my track (no-code/low-code automation) or a developer role? (2) which of my five portfolio projects to lead with, (3) the 3 keywords I must mirror in my CV, (4) my honest fit as a % with reasons.

I'm targeting [YOUR CITY/REMOTE + YOUR BACKGROUND]. List the 10 most realistic job titles for me to search for on LinkedIn and job boards, ranked by entry-friendliness for someone with a portfolio but no CS degree. For each, one line on why.

💪 Exercise

Build your job-search pipeline sheet: 15 real open roles that fit your track, sourced from LinkedIn and job boards. For each, note title, salary if listed, the portfolio project you'd lead with, and a fit score. Circle your top 5 — they're your targets for Lessons 22-23 practice.
