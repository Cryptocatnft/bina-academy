Getting Real Answers: The Four-Part Prompt

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Teach the four building blocks of a good prompt — role, context, format, examples — so the AI stops guessing what you want.
Alex · Play the lecturer

Why vague prompts fail

When you type 'plan my week' or 'fix my email,' the AI fills the gaps with guesses about an average person — and you are not average. A vague prompt is like calling your accountant and saying 'money?' and hoping for the best. The model isn't being lazy; you're making it mind-read. Every detail you skip is a coin flip. The fix isn't typing more words — it's typing the *right* words. The four-part prompt below turns a coin flip into a straight answer. Try it today on something you'd normally ask twice.

The four parts

Role — tell it who to be: 'Act as a veteran nutritionist' or 'Act as a blunt editor.' Roles pull in the right vocabulary and standards. Context — the stuff only you know: 'I cook for a family of four, hate washing dishes, 30 minutes max.' Format — what the answer should look like: a table, a 5-item list, one paragraph, a text under 100 words. Examples — show one thing you like: 'Make it punchy, like: [paste a sample].' Role aims the expertise, context aims it at *you*, format controls the deliverable, examples set the taste. Skip one and you feel it.

Before and after

Before: 'Help me plan meals.' After: 'Act as a practical family nutritionist. Context: family of four, two picky kids, no seafood, 30-minute weeknight dinners, standard supermarket. Format: 5 dinners as a table — dish, main steps, total time. Keep it under 250 words.' The second one lands on the first try, every time. Same AI, same you, ten extra seconds of typing. That's the whole trick of this course: small effort in the right place. From here on, we stack these habits — and by lesson 20, they'll run on autopilot.

The one-sentence habit

If you remember nothing else, remember this template: 'Act as ___. My situation: ___. Give me ___ in the form of ___.' Fill in the blanks, out loud if it helps. It takes about fifteen seconds and covers ninety percent of daily use. When an answer disappoints, don't start over — add one missing part. Boring answer? Tighten the role. Generic advice? Add context. Wrong shape? Fix the format. Wrong vibe? Give an example. You're not begging a machine; you're briefing a colleague. That mindset alone puts you ahead of most users out there.

Copy-ready prompt

Act as a [role: e.g., practical family nutritionist]. My situation: [context: who's involved, constraints, what you've tried]. Give me [what you want] in the form of [format: table, list, short paragraph]. Keep it under [X] words.

Before you answer, ask me up to 3 clarifying questions that would most improve your answer. Then wait for my reply.

💪 Exercise

Take one prompt you used recently that got a weak answer and rewrite it with the four parts: role, context, format, example. Run both versions and write down the two biggest differences you see in the answers.
