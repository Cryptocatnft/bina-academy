Deep Research: Your Research Assistant on Retainer

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Delegate multi-source research to Deep Research mode — brief it well, let it work, and verify the result before you trust it.
Alex · Play the lecturer

When to use it (and when not)

Deep Research exists in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity — names vary, idea is the same. Use it when the answer requires comparing many sources: big purchases, 'best of' questions, medical homework before a doctor visit, market research for a side business, school-level research for your kid. Don't use it for quick facts, simple how-tos, or anything you need in the next sixty seconds — a regular chat answers those instantly. The rule: if you'd normally open ten tabs and take notes, Deep Research is your tool. If you'd ask one friend, just chat.

Briefing your researcher

The quality of the brief decides the quality of the report. Include: the real decision behind the question ('choosing a stroller for a newborn, city sidewalks, under $600'), your constraints, what 'good' looks like ('comparison table, top 3 with tradeoffs, sources from the last 2 years'), and anything to exclude ('no US-only advice — I'm in Israel'). The tool will usually ask you a few follow-up questions before starting — answer them properly, they matter. Then let it run. Don't sit there; go do something. It pings you when it's done, with citations you can click.

Reading the report — and checking it

You'll get a structured report with links. Treat it like a bright intern's work: excellent first pass, not gospel. Scan the citations — are they real sites you recognize, or content farms? Spot-check any load-bearing claim by clicking its source, especially numbers and quotes. Watch for the classic trap: confident synthesis of shaky sources. A good habit is one follow-up message: 'Which of your claims here is least well-supported, and why?' The model is decent at flagging its own soft spots. Ten minutes of verification turns a nice report into something you'd actually act on.

Deep Research in daily life

Everyday uses people love: comparing insurance plans before renewal, researching a diagnosis before a pediatrician visit (homework, not treatment — lesson 13 covers that line), 'what should I know before signing with this property manager?,' gift research for a niche hobby, and school projects where you want your kid to see *how* research works. One warning: it can produce impressive-looking reports on topics where real expertise matters — legal, medical, financial. Use it to walk in informed, not to walk in as the expert. Informed parent beats confused parent every time.

Copy-ready prompt

Research [topic/question]. My situation: [context, constraints, budget, location]. I need this to decide: [the actual decision]. Deliver: a comparison table of the top 3-5 options with tradeoffs, key findings, and what to watch out for. Cite sources, prefer the last 2 years, and note where sources disagree.

Follow-up: 'Which claims in this report are least well-supported by your sources? Flag anything where sources conflict or evidence is thin.'

💪 Exercise

Run one Deep Research on a real open question in your life — a purchase, a plan, a decision. Brief it with the template above, let it finish, click-check two citations, and write down one thing you learned that you couldn't have found in five minutes of Googling.
