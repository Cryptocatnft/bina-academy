Custom Instructions: Set It Once, It Works Everywhere

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Configure your AI's permanent settings so every conversation already knows who you are and how you like answers.
Alex · Play the lecturer

What Custom Instructions actually are

Custom Instructions is a settings panel — in ChatGPT it's under Settings → Personalization → Custom Instructions; Claude and Gemini have their own versions of persistent preferences. It's two boxes: 'What should the AI know about you?' and 'How should it respond?' Whatever you write there rides along into every new conversation. The key mental model: it's the difference between calling a stranger for help every time versus having a colleague who already knows your world. You write it once, it applies everywhere, and you can edit it any time. Today you'll write yours for real.

What to put in it

Box one — the facts the AI can't guess: family situation, job in plain words, town, key constraints ('budget-conscious,' 'kosher kitchen,' 'I manage a small dental clinic'). Box two — your taste in answers: 'Plain English, no corporate fluff, short paragraphs, bullet points over essays, give concrete examples, tell me when my idea is bad.' Notice that last one — you're allowed to order honesty. Keep the whole thing under about 300 words each; it's a memo, not a memoir. Update it whenever life changes — new job, new baby, new diet. It should always be a little out of date, not a lot.

The line between useful and clutter

Here's the trade-off: every word in your instructions colors every answer. Great for stable facts ('I live in Denver'), risky for moods ('I'm overwhelmed this week') — moods belong in the chat itself. Also skip anything that's really a security question, which we'll cover in lesson 19 — think twice before pasting full ID numbers or account credentials into a settings box. The sweet spot: durable facts and strong style preferences. Vague adjectives like 'be helpful and nice' waste space — the model does that anyway. Specific, checkable, current. That's a good profile.

Checking your work

After you save, run the acid test: open a brand-new chat and ask something ordinary — 'plan a Saturday for my family' or 'draft a message to my kid's teacher.' If the answer already fits you — right length, right tone, knows the constraints — your instructions are working. If it still feels generic, go back and add what was missing. This loop — set, test, tweak — is how you tune the system. Most people set it once and forget it; you'll revisit it every couple of months. Congratulations: your AI now starts every conversation halfway to knowing you.

Copy-ready prompt

Box 1 (What should the AI know about you?) — example: 'I'm a parent of two (ages 7 and 10) in Denver. I run a small home-services business. Budget-conscious, time-poor. Non-technical: plain English only.'

Box 2 (How should it respond?) — example: 'Short and direct, no corporate fluff. Bullet points and tables over essays. Concrete examples, not theory. If my request is unclear, ask one clarifying question before answering. Tell me when my idea won't work.'
