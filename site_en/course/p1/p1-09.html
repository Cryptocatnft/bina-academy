Smart Weekly Planning

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Run a ten-minute weekly planning session with the AI that sorts your obligations, protects your priorities, and produces a schedule you'll actually follow.
Alex · Play the lecturer

The brain dump

Start messy — that's the point. One message, no structure: everything in your head. Work deadlines, the birthday gift, the car inspection, 'I should call my sister,' the school bake sale, that back pain you keep ignoring. Don't organize; just empty. Then add the fixed points: 'Monday and Wednesday I have evening commitments, Thursday the kids are with grandma, Saturday we're at the game.' Fixed points first because they're the walls of the week — everything else has to fit around them, not through them. Two minutes of dumping, and the AI has the full swamp in view.

The sort and the schedule

Now the instruction: 'Sort this into: appointments with times, tasks, and things I'm pretending to do. Build a realistic week around my fixed points — one or two big tasks per day max, not five. Flag conflicts and tell me what doesn't fit.' The key words are *realistic* and *doesn't fit*. The AI will happily build you a fantasy schedule of eight-hour productive days unless you forbid it — so forbid it. Ask for a simple day-by-day layout with time blocks, and have it protect one thing for you: 'reserve Tuesday evening for something that's just mine.' A plan without a reserve is a countdown to burnout, and you didn't build this system for that.

The Sunday ritual

Make it a standing appointment, ten minutes, same time weekly. Save the chat (or run it in your 'Family HQ' project from lesson 3) so each week starts from the last one: 'Here's what actually happened last week — the dentist got moved again, the report took twice as long. Rebuild this week knowing that.' The AI learns your real pace: that a 'quick errand' costs you two hours, that Wednesday is always chaos. Over a month, your plans get honest. Midweek correction is allowed and encouraged: a two-minute 'everything slipped, what do I drop?' beats silently abandoning the plan by Thursday.

Family upgrade

Level two: run the week as a household. Share the ritual with your partner, or paste both sets of constraints and let the AI find the gaps: 'Who's picking up the kids Tuesday? You double-booked Thursday.' Ask for a shared shopping-and-errands list and one family fun slot that actually gets defended. Two ground rules keep it sane: keep individual work tasks at whatever granularity you like, but family commitments go in with times and owners; and the AI proposes — humans decide. It's a planning assistant, not a family court. Even so, an assistant that remembers all the moving parts beats the old whiteboard, every single week.

Copy-ready prompt

Here's everything in my head for this week (unsorted): [brain dump]. Fixed points I can't move: [days/times]. Sort it into appointments, tasks, and things I'm pretending to do. Build a realistic week — max 1-2 big tasks per day, protect one evening for me, flag conflicts, and tell me honestly what's not going to fit.

Midweek fix: 'Here's what actually happened: [what slipped]. Rebuild the rest of the week — what do I drop, what do I move, what stays?'

💪 Exercise

Do the full ten-minute session today: brain dump, sort, realistic schedule. Then put a recurring 10-minute 'plan the week' block in your calendar for the same time next week — the ritual only pays if it repeats.
