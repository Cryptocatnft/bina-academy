Content That Sounds Like YOU

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Teach the AI your authentic voice and produce writing — posts, updates, captions — that people who know you would recognize as yours.
Alex · Play the lecturer

The voice sample

The core move: paste 2-3 things you genuinely wrote — texts, emails, a post, even voicemail transcripts. Then: 'Here are three samples of my writing. Extract my voice: average sentence length, vocabulary level, humor style, how I open and close, what I never say. Summarize the profile as rules, then wait.' What comes back is often a small revelation — 'I use a lot of dashes, I'm warmer than I think, I end with questions.' Keep that profile. Save it in a doc, put it in Custom Instructions (lesson 2), or paste it at the start of writing chats. Voice is now a reusable asset, not a miracle you beg for each time.

Killing the AI-isms

Every model has tells: 'delve,' 'tapestry,' 'it's not just X, it's Y,' 'game-changer,' the em-dash avalanche, the closing paragraph that summarizes with fake gravity. Add to your voice profile: 'Banned words: delve, tapestry, unlock, elevate, game-changer. No 'it's not just X, it's Y' constructions. Vary sentence length — some short. Like this. No summarizing final paragraph.' Then the edit trick: 'Read this draft and flag anything that sounds like generic AI writing — be brutal.' Two passes of that and the draft starts sounding like a person. A specific, slightly weird, recognizably-you person.

From idea to post

The realistic content workflow, for the business owner or the family-update writer alike: brain-dump the idea in your own ugly words — 'want to post about the renovation being finally done, mostly grateful to my wife, also the painter story' — then: 'Here's my voice profile and my raw notes. Draft [a post / an email / a caption]. One draft, in my voice, no AI-isms.' One draft matters: asking for 'options' gets you committee-speak. Then edit by reading it out loud — if you stumble, it's not you yet. Small daily habit: keep a note of things you said out loud that sounded like you. That's your voice corpus, and it only grows.

The honesty rule

One boundary keeps this healthy: your name goes on it, so the substance must be yours. The AI arranges your words; it doesn't have your memories, your opinions, or the story of the painter. When a draft makes a claim — 'we tripled our output,' 'the trip changed me' — check that it's true. And notice the flip side of this skill: now that you know how AI-isms sound, you'll spot machine text everywhere (you'll never unsee 'delve'). Use that radar on your own output. When your kid's teacher, your clients, and your mother all say 'that sounds exactly like you' — that's the certificate for this lesson. Hang it proudly.

Copy-ready prompt

Here are three samples of my writing: [paste]. Extract my voice as rules: sentence length, vocabulary, humor, openings/closings, words I never use. Then wait for my next instruction.

Banned in all drafts: delve, tapestry, unlock, elevate, game-changer, 'it's not just X, it's Y.' Vary sentence length. No summarizing final paragraph. If a sentence sounds like generic AI writing, cut it.

My voice profile: [paste profile]. Raw notes: [your messy brain-dump]. Draft one [post / email / caption] in my voice. One draft only, no options, no AI-isms.

💪 Exercise

Build your voice profile today: paste 3 of your own messages, extract the rules, save them where you'll reuse them. Then write one real thing — a post, a family update, a client email — through the profile, read it out loud, and edit until it stumbles nowhere.
