The Family Budget, Finally

🎯 What you'll be able to do: Turn your real spending data — bank exports and receipts — into a clear picture of where money goes, and get concrete options for the leaks.
Alex · Play the lecturer

Getting your data in

Most banks let you export a statement as CSV — that's the gold standard. No CSV? Two solid alternatives: paste the transactions as text straight into the chat, or go low-tech: photograph the statement like lesson 6. Three months is the magic window — one month lies (there was a wedding, a holiday), three months tells the truth. Before pasting, do one safety pass: the transaction list is all the AI needs. Blur or delete account numbers and any digits you wouldn't text a friend. Money habits are personal — treat the data like it is. Lesson 19 covers this in depth; today, just crop before you paste.

The clear picture

Prompt: 'Categorize these three months of transactions: housing, food, kids, transport, subscriptions, debt, fun, other. Show monthly averages, flag anything that surprises you, and estimate what's fixed versus changeable.' What comes back is usually an eye-opener: the subscription count (nobody has four streaming services on purpose), the food number (delivery versus groceries versus 'just coffee'), the 'other' monster. This is where the feelings come in — people see a number and feel judged. Don't. It's a map, not a grade. You can't steer what you can't see, and now you can see it.

The leaks and the fixes

Now the fun part — options. 'Based on this data, show me the 5 cheapest-to-fix leaks, ranked by savings per month, with the exact first step for each.' The AI is good at this because it has no embarrassment: cancel the gym you visited twice, make the subscription audit list, negotiate the phone bill (lesson 12 gives you the script), set the grocery plan from lesson 6. Ask it to separate the big structural stuff (housing, car, childcare — change slowly, with planning) from the everyday stuff (change this week, painlessly). Attacking the big structural items first is how people burn out. Start with the leaks.

Making it stick

A budget you build once and never revisit is a museum piece. The maintenance version is painless: a monthly 15-minute check-in. 'Here's this month's transactions. Compare to our averages: what changed, what crept up, are we still on track with the two things we said we'd fix?' Keep the original chat or a 'Money' project so the context carries over. Two rules keep it honest: the AI estimates — always cross-check anything that looks weird against the actual statement — and for big decisions (investments, taxes, debt restructuring), the AI is your homework partner, not your advisor. Map first, then steer.

Copy-ready prompt

Here are 3 months of bank transactions: [paste or attach]. Categorize them (housing / food / kids / transport / subscriptions / debt / fun / other), show monthly averages, separate fixed from changeable, and flag anything surprising. No judgment, just clarity.

Based on that analysis: list the 5 cheapest-to-fix leaks, ranked by monthly savings, with the exact first step for each. Then one paragraph: which big structural item deserves a serious look this year?

Monthly check-in: 'Here's this month's transactions: [paste]. Compare to our averages — what changed, what crept up, are we on track with what we said we'd fix?'

💪 Exercise

Export (or photograph) one month of transactions, run the categorization prompt, and act on exactly one finding today — cancel one subscription or send one negotiation draft (see lesson 12). One leak, closed, for real.
